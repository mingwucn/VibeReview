# C210 — Candidate-Claim Normalization (C210@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Normalize and cluster the raw candidate claims into a clean, non-redundant claim
set with an explicit mapping back to the raw candidates. Normalization removes
duplication and overlap without losing information: every raw candidate remains
traceable, and every consequential merge or split passes a human gate.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the full set of raw candidate claims (all sections) and the research outline
   (for section structure and claim placement). Work across sections as well as
   within them: duplication and overlap frequently appear where section reports
   covered neighboring territory.
2. Normalize each raw candidate: fix garbled statement text, standardize
   terminology against the outline's terms, and make the scope statement explicit
   where the raw claim left it implicit. Do not change the assertion itself
   during normalization; statement edits are limited to clarity and terminology.
   Record any normalization that touched an assertion's meaning and flag it for
   human review.
3. Compare every pair of normalized claims and assign exactly one relationship
   from the fixed set — DUPLICATE_OF, NARROWS, BROADENS, OVERLAPS, CONTRADICTS,
   RELATED_MECHANISM — or none. The meanings are:
   - DUPLICATE_OF: the same assertion at the same scope.
   - NARROWS: one claim is a scope-restricted version of the other.
   - BROADENS: one claim is a scope-generalized version of the other.
   - OVERLAPS: the claims share an assertion but neither contains the other.
   - CONTRADICTS: the claims cannot both hold as stated; record which reading
     creates the conflict.
   - RELATED_MECHANISM: the claims describe different mechanisms plausibly
     behind the same phenomenon.
4. Cluster claims by relationship: build the normalized claim set by collapsing
   DUPLICATE_OF chains to a single representative, folding NARROWS/BROADENS
   pairs into the appropriately scoped claim, and joining OVERLAPS pairs only
   where the joined statement remains atomic.
5. Keep CONTRADICTS and RELATED_MECHANISM relationships visible: contradictory
   claims are not merged or dropped; they remain in the normalized set with
   their conflict recorded, since contradictions drive challenge and synthesis.
6. Mark every merge, split, or scope change as consequential if it alters what a
   claim asserts or what evidence would bear on it. Consequential merges and
   splits require human gate approval before they take effect; group them in the
   output for one-pass review. Non-consequential merges (pure duplicates with
   identical scope) may proceed without a gate.
7. Produce the claim mapping: for every normalized claim, list the raw candidate
   IDs it subsumes and the relationship chain that produced it. Every raw
   candidate ID must appear in exactly one mapping entry.
8. Check the normalized set for scope consistency against the outline: every
   claim must belong to exactly one section, and claims that span sections must
   be flagged for the cross-section synthesis stage rather than silently
   double-counted.
9. Do not drop contradictory, weak, or odd claims to tidy the set; flag them.
   Do not invent claims to fill perceived gaps.

## Output Contract

```text
NORMALIZED CLAIM SET

Normalized claims:
- Normalized claim ID: <N-nnn>
  Statement: <single atomic assertion>
  Section ID: <owning section>
  Scope: <explicit scope>
  Origin run(s): <Deep Research run IDs>
  Status: <clean | flag: <reason>>
- ...

CLAIM MAPPING

For each normalized claim:
- Normalized claim ID: <N-nnn>
  Subsumes raw candidates: <raw ID list>
  Relationship chain: <raw A DUPLICATE_OF raw B -> N-nnn; ...>

Unmapped raw candidates: none (every raw candidate appears exactly once)

Consequential changes requiring human gate:
- <change>: <merge/split/scope change> | affects: <claim IDs> | assertion changed: yes/no
- ...

Cross-section flags:
- <claim ID>: spans sections <IDs>; route to cross-section synthesis
```

- Relationships are exactly: DUPLICATE_OF, NARROWS, BROADENS, OVERLAPS,
  CONTRADICTS, RELATED_MECHANISM.
- Every raw candidate ID appears in exactly one mapping entry.
- Contradictory claims remain in the set with their conflict recorded.
- Consequential merges and splits are grouped for explicit human approval.
