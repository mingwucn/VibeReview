# C220 — Candidate-Claim Challenge (C220@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Challenge every normalized candidate claim before it enters retrieval and
synthesis. The challenge probes each claim for the weaknesses that most often
corrupt review conclusions: overbroad scope, causal overreach, missing
populations, single-study dependence, and unconsidered counter-evidence. Its
output is a per-claim risk profile that synthesis must respect.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the normalized claim set, the claim mapping, the outline (for scope
   boundaries), and the compiled Deep Research material the claims came from.
   Judge every claim against these components, not against outside knowledge.
2. For each normalized claim, identify the strongest counter-evidence lead:
   the most plausible study type, finding, or line of work that could contradict
   or qualify the claim, based on what the compiled discovery material already
   hints at or omits. State the lead concretely enough to become a retrieval
   intent.
3. Check scope overreach: compare the claim's stated scope against its source
   material. Flag any claim whose scope generalizes beyond the processes,
   materials, machines, datasets, or regimes the source material covers.
4. Check causal overreach: flag any claim whose wording asserts or implies
   causation stronger than its source material demonstrates, and rephrase the
   risk as a caution for synthesis, not as a silent edit.
5. Check for missing populations and materials: enumerate the materials,
   machines, geometries, datasets, process regimes, and application domains the
   claim could be read to cover but the source material does not include.
6. Check dependence on single studies: flag every claim resting on one study or
   one research group as a concentration risk, and state what independent
   confirmation would look like.
7. Assign each challenge finding a severity (claim-threatening, scope-limiting,
   monitor) and record which synthesis handling it implies: mandatory
   contradiction intent in retrieval, mandatory boundary intent, qualification
   wording, or no change.
8. Do not modify the claims. This prompt produces challenges only; claim text
   changes happen in synthesis with full traceability.
9. Do not suppress challenges to protect the emerging narrative. A claim with no
   found weaknesses must still record its checked dimensions explicitly.

## Output Contract

```text
CLAIM CHALLENGE REPORT

Claim: <normalized claim ID> — <statement>

- Strongest counter-evidence lead: <concrete lead, usable as a retrieval intent>
- Scope overreach: <none | <specific overreach>>
- Causal overreach: <none | <specific overreach caution>>
- Missing populations/materials: <none | <enumerated list>>
- Single-study dependence: <none | <study/group and what confirmation looks like>>
- Severity summary: <claim-threatening | scope-limiting | monitor>
- Synthesis handling: <mandatory contradiction intent | mandatory boundary intent |
  qualification wording | no change>

<repeat per normalized claim>

Cross-claim observations:
- <contradictions between normalized claims that challenge stages must keep visible>
```

- Every normalized claim receives all six challenge dimensions; "none" must be
  stated explicitly, with what was checked.
- Every challenge names its severity and the synthesis handling it implies.
- No claim text is modified in this stage.
