# C200 — Atomic Candidate-Claim Generation (C200@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Generate atomic candidate claims from the compiled outline section and its Deep
Research reports. Candidate claims are the raw material of the review's
synthesis: they convert discovery prose into discrete, checkable assertions that
retrieval and later evidence assessment can work against. They are
discovery-derived only and are not yet evidence-authorized.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the exact outline section (its scope statement bounds what claims may be
   generated) and the Deep Research reports originating from this section.
   Confine claim generation to claims the reports actually support as leads; do
   not import claims from outside the compiled components.
2. Extract every substantive assertion the reports make or suggest about the
   section's topic. Candidate claims come only from the compiled Deep Research
   reports, not from the model's own knowledge; if the reports are silent on a
   topic, no claim may be generated for it.
3. Make every claim atomic: one substantive assertion per claim. If a sentence
   contains two assertions — for example a finding plus its explanation, or an
   effect plus the conditions under which it holds — split it into separate
   claims. Do not let one claim carry a conjunction of independently checkable
   statements.
4. Give every claim an explicit scope: the process family, AI task, materials,
   machines, datasets, regimes, and period the claim is about. Scope must be
   narrow enough that evidence can confirm or deny the claim as stated, and must
   not silently generalize beyond the reports' statements.
5. Use restrained causal language. Prefer associative wording (correlates with,
   is associated with, predicts, improves measured outcome under stated
   conditions) unless the compiled reports state a demonstrated causal
   mechanism, and even then keep the causal claim no stronger than the reports
   make it. Flag any claim whose causal wording outruns its source report.
6. Classify every claim by claim type using the taxonomy the project uses (for
   example: empirical finding, methodological observation, comparative
   assessment, outlook statement). Mark outlook or speculative statements as
   such and never phrase them as established results.
7. Attach to every claim the Deep Research run origin (the run ID it was
   extracted from) and the source references the run cited for it. Carry the
   references as unverified leads exactly as the report states them; do not
   normalize, complete, or invent bibliographic details.
8. Give every claim retrieval hints: concrete search leads for the later
   retrieval stage — key terms and variants, likely venues or study types,
   author groups or lines of work the report names, and suggested intents
   (support, contradiction, boundary, alternative, method challenge, null
   result). Write retrieval hints so that a retrieval operator who has not read
   the report can construct queries from them.
9. Record every extracted claim even when it is weak, marginal, or in tension
   with other claims; the challenge and normalization stages handle those. Do
   not rank, filter, or merge at this stage.
10. Close by stating explicitly that the output is discovery-derived only:
    candidate claims are not evidence-authorized, and none may be treated as
    established review conclusions.

## Output Contract

```text
CANDIDATE CLAIMS — <section ID>

For each claim:
- Claim ID: <section ID>-C<nnn>
- Statement: <one substantive assertion only>
- Section ID: <owning outline section>
- Deep Research run origin: <run ID this claim was extracted from>
- Source references: <references as given by the run, unverified leads>
- Claim type: <empirical finding | methodological observation | comparative assessment | outlook statement | ...>
- Scope: <processes, AI task, materials, machines, datasets, regimes, period>
- Retrieval hints:
  - key terms and variants: ...
  - likely venues / study types: ...
  - named author groups or lines of work: ...
  - suggested intents: support / contradiction / boundary / alternative / method_challenge / null_result

<repeat per claim>

DISCOVERY-DERIVED STATEMENT:
These candidate claims are discovery-derived only. They are not
evidence-authorized and must not be treated as established conclusions.
```

- Every claim is atomic: exactly one substantive assertion per statement.
- Every claim carries all eight fields above; none may be blank.
- Causal language must be restrained relative to the source report; claims whose
  causal wording outruns the report must be flagged.
- Retrieval hints must be concrete enough to construct queries from.
