# M430 — Controlled Prose Rendering (M430@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Render passing propositions into section prose under strict control. Rendering
turns audited propositions into readable scientific text while changing nothing
about what the review asserts: the propositions fix the scientific content, and
this prompt governs only the transformation into sentences.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the section contract (authorized ClaimPackets, required contradiction,
   required qualification, known uncertainty, prohibited assertions) and the
   propositions that passed the proposition audit for this section. Render only
   passing propositions; a failed proposition produces no prose.
2. Render each proposition into one or more sentences that state exactly the
   proposition — no more and no less. Keep every proposition's scope,
   certainty, and causal wording identical to the audited proposition.
3. You may improve only the surface of the text: syntax, flow, readability,
   terminology, and paragraph transitions. Use consistent terminology from the
   outline and focus modules; connect propositions with transitions that make
   the section's argument legible; write in the voice of a critical narrative
   review.
4. Prohibit introducing new scientific propositions: do not add assertions,
   findings, mechanisms, explanations, examples, numbers, comparisons, or
   caveats that are not present in the audited propositions and their citation
   bindings. Every sentence must be a rendering of an audited proposition or a
   purely organizational connective (signposting, cross-reference, transition)
   that asserts nothing scientific.
5. Preserve the contract's required representation: the required contradiction,
   required qualification, and known uncertainty must appear in the rendered
   prose with the strength the contract states, not softened by rendering.
6. Bind citations as the propositions specify: every rendered sentence carries
   the citation bindings of the proposition it renders, and no sentence gains
   or loses a citation at rendering time.
7. Mark every substantive rendered sentence with the ID of the proposition it
   renders, so the sentence audit can check entailment proposition by
   proposition.
8. Review the rendered section against the contract's prohibited assertions:
   if any sentence pattern matches a prohibition, rewrite the sentence to
   comply or drop it and record the drop.
9. Do not merge propositions into overpacked sentences to improve flow; prefer
   splitting. Do not resolve tensions between propositions by wording; the
   tension is content and belongs to the propositions.

## Output Contract

```text
RENDERED SECTION — <manuscript section ID and title>

<Paragraph 1>
[Sentence 1] (proposition: P-nn) <citation bindings>
[Sentence 2] (proposition: P-nn) <citation bindings>

<Paragraph 2>
...

Rendering check:
- Every substantive sentence maps to exactly one audited proposition: confirmed
- New scientific propositions introduced: none
- Contract-required contradiction, qualification, uncertainty present: confirmed
- Citation bindings unchanged from propositions: confirmed
- Prohibited-assertion patterns: none matched
```

- Only passing propositions are rendered.
- Permitted improvements are limited to syntax, flow, readability, terminology,
  and paragraph transitions.
- Introducing new scientific propositions or claims not present in the audited
  propositions is prohibited; the check must record none.
- Every substantive sentence is annotated with its proposition ID and citation
  bindings.
