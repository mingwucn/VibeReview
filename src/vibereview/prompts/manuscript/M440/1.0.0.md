# M440 — Proposition Audit (M440@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Audit generated propositions against their section contract and authorized
ClaimPackets. The audit is the gate between proposition generation and prose
rendering: a proposition that fails here never reaches prose, so no wording
fix downstream can smuggle unauthorized content into the manuscript.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the section contract, the authorized ClaimPackets with their synthesis
   records, and the propositions to audit. Audit every proposition
   individually; do not sample.
2. Check classification: does the proposition's assigned classification match
   what it actually asserts (finding, comparative assessment, methodological
   observation, limitation, outlook)? A misclassified proposition fails.
3. Check ClaimPacket authorization: does every supporting ClaimPacket the
   proposition relies on belong to this section's authorized list? Any reliance
   on an unauthorized, rejected, or foreign packet fails the proposition.
4. Check scope: is the proposition's scope identical to, or narrower than, the
   combined scope of its supporting packets? Any generalization beyond the
   packets' boundary conditions fails.
5. Check certainty: does the proposition's certainty level match the weakest of
   its supporting packets? Any proposition stated more confidently than its
   evidence fails.
6. Check citation binding: for every claim the proposition makes, is there at
   least one supporting packet whose evidence can be cited for it, and are the
   required citation bindings from generation present? A proposition with
   unbindable content fails.
7. Check for unsupported additions: does the proposition assert anything — a
   mechanism, an explanation, an example, a number, a comparison, a caveat —
   that does not appear in its supporting packets or the section contract?
   Any unsupported addition fails, however small.
8. Check for overstatement: compare the proposition against the contract's
   prohibited assertions and against restrained-causal-language requirements.
   Any match fails; near misses are flagged with the required rewrite.
9. Check contract coverage: the section's required contradiction, required
   qualification, and known uncertainty must each be represented by some
   passing proposition. If they are missing, the audit fails the set, not
   individual propositions, and says what is missing.
10. Assign each proposition PASS or FAIL. For FAIL, state the exact check
    failed, the offending text, and the minimal correction that would pass. Do
    not correct passing propositions; output them unchanged.
11. Only passing propositions proceed to controlled prose rendering; record the
    proceeding set explicitly.

## Output Contract

```text
PROPOSITION AUDIT — <manuscript section ID and title>

Proposition P-nn: <statement>
- Classification check: pass | fail (<why>)
- ClaimPacket authorization check: pass | fail (<offending reliance>)
- Scope check: pass | fail (<overreach>)
- Certainty check: pass | fail (<overconfidence>)
- Citation binding check: pass | fail (<unbindable content>)
- Unsupported-addition check: pass | fail (<addition>)
- Overstatement check: pass | fail (<prohibited pattern matched>)
Verdict: PASS | FAIL
If FAIL, minimal correction: <exact required change>

<repeat per proposition>

Contract coverage check:
- Required contradiction represented by a passing proposition: yes/no
- Required qualification represented by a passing proposition: yes/no
- Known uncertainty represented by a passing proposition: yes/no

Proceeding to rendering (passing propositions only): <list of proposition IDs>
```

- Every proposition is audited against all seven checks.
- Verdicts are exactly PASS or FAIL; every FAIL names the check, the offending
  text, and the minimal correction.
- Only passing propositions are listed as proceeding to rendering.
