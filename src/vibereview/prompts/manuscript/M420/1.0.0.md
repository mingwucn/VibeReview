# M420 — Proposition Generation (M420@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Generate the audited propositions for one manuscript section from its section
contract and authorized ClaimPackets only. Propositions are the atomic units of
manuscript content: each states approximately one substantive scientific
assertion, and only propositions that pass audit may later be rendered into
prose. This stage creates no prose and cites no evidence directly.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the section contract (purpose, authorized ClaimPackets, required
   contradiction, required qualification, known uncertainty, prohibited
   assertions) and the full text of the authorized ClaimPackets. Use nothing
   outside these components.
2. Cover the contract's purpose with propositions: plan the set of propositions
   that together argue the section's purpose, each proposition approximately
   one substantive scientific assertion. A proposition may bundle only what a
   reader grasps as one claim; if it takes an "and" or "but" to state, split it.
3. Derive each proposition from authorized ClaimPackets only. Every
   proposition must trace to at least one authorized ClaimPacket; state the
   packet IDs for each. No proposition may rest on rejected claims, on evidence
   outside the section's authorization, or on the model's own knowledge.
4. Respect packet content exactly: match each proposition's scope, certainty,
   and causal wording to its supporting packets. Where a packet is qualified,
   the proposition carries the qualification; where a packet is tentative, the
   proposition is tentative.
5. Represent the contract's required elements as propositions: the required
   contradiction must appear as a proposition (or be visibly woven into one);
   the required qualification and the known uncertainty must each be visible
   in the proposition set.
6. Check every proposition against the contract's prohibited assertions: no
   proposition may match a prohibited pattern in scope, causality, certainty,
   or transfer claim. Remove or rewrite violating propositions at this stage,
   recording the reason.
7. Assign each proposition a classification that the proposition audit will
   check (for example: finding, comparative assessment, methodological
   observation, limitation, outlook), and note the citation bindings the
   proposition will require in prose — which packets' evidence sentences must
   be citable when this proposition is rendered.
8. Keep the total in proportion to the section: generate the propositions the
   contract's purpose requires and no filler. Do not pad thin sections with
   restatements, and do not compress rich sections into single overpacked
   propositions.
9. Do not render prose. Do not copy evidence text into propositions. Do not
   take the raw evidence-to-prose shortcut: packets to propositions to audited
   prose is the only path.

## Output Contract

```text
PROPOSITIONS — <manuscript section ID and title>

Proposition P-nn:
- Statement: <approximately one substantive scientific assertion>
- Classification: <finding | comparative assessment | methodological observation | limitation | outlook | ...>
- Supporting ClaimPackets: <packet IDs>
- Scope: <as bounded by the packets>
- Certainty: <as bounded by the packets>
- Required citation bindings: <which packet evidence must be citable>
- Represents (if applicable): required contradiction | required qualification | known uncertainty

<repeat per proposition>

Coverage check:
- Section purpose covered: confirmed
- Required contradiction represented: confirmed
- Required qualification represented: confirmed
- Known uncertainty represented: confirmed
- No prohibited-assertion pattern matched: confirmed
```

- Every proposition traces to at least one authorized ClaimPacket.
- No proposition's scope, certainty, or causal wording exceeds its packets.
- No prose and no raw evidence text appears in the output.
