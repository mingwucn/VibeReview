# S310 — Cross-Section Claim Synthesis (S310@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Synthesize claims that span multiple outline sections. After per-claim
synthesis, the review can still hold contradictory, redundant, or mis-scoped
claims across section boundaries. This prompt reconciles the claim set
globally: it resolves redundancy, surfaces tension, and identifies emergent
cross-cutting themes that no single-section synthesis can see.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the per-claim synthesis outputs for all sections, the normalized claim set
   with its cross-section flags, the claim challenge reports, and the research
   outline (for section boundaries and cross-cutting themes).
2. Build the cross-section claim map: list every claim flagged as spanning
   sections, every pair of claims in different sections that overlap in
   assertion or scope, and every contradiction recorded between claims of
   different sections.
3. Detect redundancy across sections. Where two sections would assert the same
   synthesized conclusion, decide the single home section for the full
   statement and reduce the other to a cross-reference. Record what was
   deduplicated and where the authoritative statement lives.
4. Detect tension across sections. Where claims from different sections
   conflict — different conclusions at overlapping scope, incompatible
   certainty levels, or contradictory boundary conditions — reconcile them
   explicitly: narrow one or both scopes, align certainty with the weaker
   evidence, or record an unresolved tension with the deciding evidence named.
   Do not let two sections stand on incompatible statements.
5. Detect emergent cross-cutting themes: patterns that appear only across
   sections — for example a methodological weakness recurring in every process
   family, a generalization barrier common to several AI tasks, or a data
   limitation spanning the corpus. For each theme, name it, list the claims
   evidencing it, and state whether the outline's cross-cutting sections
   already capture it or the manuscript outline must make room for it.
6. Check scope consistency of cross-section claims: a claim appearing in two
   sections must carry one scope, not two; align scopes to the narrowest
   evidence and record the alignment.
7. Apply the same evidential discipline as per-claim synthesis: no vote
   counting across sections — the number of sections repeating a statement is
   not evidence for it — and no omission of contradictory or qualifying
   evidence to make cross-section narratives tidy.
8. Where cross-section reconciliation changes a synthesized claim's wording,
   scope, or certainty, record the change with its motivating evidence; changed
   claims follow the same revalidation requirement as revised claims.
9. Output the reconciled cross-section claim set with every change traceable;
   do not silently edit per-section synthesis results.

## Output Contract

```text
CROSS-SECTION SYNTHESIS

Cross-section claim map:
- <claim ID>: sections <IDs> | relationship: <overlap | contradiction | shared scope | cross-cutting theme>
- ...

Redundancy resolutions:
- <claim IDs>: authoritative statement lives in section <ID>; reduced to
  cross-reference in section <ID>

Tension reconciliations:
- <claim IDs>: <narrowed scope | aligned certainty | unresolved with deciding evidence>
- ...

Emergent cross-cutting themes:
- <theme>: evidenced by claims <IDs>; <already captured by section X | manuscript
  outline must make room>

Scope alignments:
- <claim ID>: scope aligned to <narrowest evidence statement>

Evidence discipline:
- Vote counting across sections: prohibited and not used.
- Contradictory/qualifying evidence: none omitted.

Changed claims requiring revalidation:
- <claim ID>: <change and motivating evidence>
```

- Every cross-section overlap, contradiction, and shared scope is listed in the
  map.
- Every redundancy resolution names the authoritative home section.
- Every tension is either reconciled with a recorded mechanism or explicitly
  unresolved with deciding evidence named.
- No per-section synthesis result is edited silently.
