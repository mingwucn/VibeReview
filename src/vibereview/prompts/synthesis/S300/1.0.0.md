# S300 — Evidence-Constrained Claim Synthesis (S300@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Synthesize each claim against its complete aggregated evidence and decide the
claim's fate. Synthesis is where the review's evidentiary discipline is enforced:
the decision must reflect the quality, directness, and independence of the
evidence — not its volume — and must account for every piece of evidence
attached to the claim, including what argues against it.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the complete paper-level evidence aggregation for the claim (every
   ClaimPaperEvidence record), the retrieval coverage for the claim, the source
   quality records of the underlying papers, and the known study dependence
   information. Do not synthesize from partial evidence: if the aggregation is
   incomplete, stop and say what is missing.
2. Inventory the evidence before judging. For each contributing paper, note its
   canonical relation to the claim (supports, contradicts, qualifies,
   contextual, unclear), its methodological relevance, its directness, and its
   source-quality state. Papers whose source quality does not permit them to
   authorize evidence must not count toward any conclusion.
3. Establish study independence. Identify which papers share datasets, authors,
   methods, or experimental platforms; dependent studies count as one
   evidential source, never as independent confirmations.
4. Prohibit vote counting explicitly and absolutely: do not count papers as
   votes, do not weight conclusions by citation count or study count, and do
   not treat three weak studies as outweighing one strong direct study. The
   decision must be argued from directness, methodological relevance, strength,
   scope match, and independence of the evidence — never from its quantity.
5. Prohibit omitting contradictory or qualifying evidence: every contradicts,
   qualifies, and unclear relation in the aggregation must be addressed in the
   synthesis reasoning, and none may be dropped, downgraded, or footnoted away
   because it is inconvenient. If contradictory evidence exists, the synthesis
   states how the claim accommodates it or why the claim fails.
6. Judge the claim against the evidence: does the evidence, taken as a whole
   and at its strongest honest reading, support the claim as stated? Consider
   directness, scope match, methodological relevance, generalization of the
   evidence beyond its conditions, heterogeneity across studies, contradictions,
   boundary conditions, study independence, and source quality.
7. Assign exactly one outcome:
   - RETAIN: the claim holds as stated against the complete evidence.
   - NARROW: the claim holds only under restricted scope; state the restricted
     scope exactly.
   - REVISE: the claim's assertion must change; state the revised wording and
     the evidence motivating it. Revised claims require revalidation before
     they may proceed.
   - REJECT: the evidence does not support the claim; state the deciding
     evidence.
   - UNCLEAR: the evidence is insufficient, too dependent, or too mixed to
     decide; state what evidence would settle it.
8. Record a certainty level for the outcome (for example established, probable,
   tentative, contested) justified by evidence strength and independence.
9. Carry forward, for every non-REJECT outcome, the contradictions,
   qualifications, and boundary conditions that prose rendering will be required
   to represent; synthesis output without them is incomplete.
10. Where the synthesis involves a major interpretive judgment, mark the item
    for the human gate instead of resolving it silently.

## Output Contract

```text
CLAIM SYNTHESIS — <claim ID>

Claim: <statement>
Evidence inventory:
- <paper>: <relation> | methodological relevance: ... | directness: ... | source quality: ...
- ...

Study independence assessment:
- <independent source groups and which papers cluster>

Contradictory and qualifying evidence addressed:
- <every contradicts/qualifies/unclear relation and how it is accommodated>

Vote-counting prohibition: observed — the decision is argued from evidence
quality, directness, and independence, never from study count.
Contradiction-omission prohibition: observed — no contradictory or qualifying
evidence was omitted.

Outcome: RETAIN | NARROW | REVISE | REJECT | UNCLEAR
Outcome rationale: <reasoning over the complete evidence>
Restricted scope (if NARROW): <exact restricted scope>
Revised wording (if REVISE): <exact new wording and motivation>
Deciding evidence (if REJECT): <the evidence that decides>
Missing evidence (if UNCLEAR): <what would settle it>
Certainty: <established | probable | tentative | contested>
Required representation in prose:
- Contradictions: ...
- Qualifications: ...
- Boundary conditions: ...
Human gate (if major interpretation): <item and question>
```

- Outcomes are exactly: RETAIN, NARROW, REVISE, REJECT, UNCLEAR.
- REVISE outcomes state exact revised wording; revised claims require
  revalidation.
- Every contradicts/qualifies/unclear relation in the aggregation is addressed
  in the reasoning.
- The vote-counting and contradiction-omission prohibitions must each be
  explicitly recorded as observed.
