# P030 — Outline Challenge (P030@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Challenge the provisional research outline item by item before any Deep Research
runs are committed against it. The challenge audits every section and subsection
against the scoping discovery and the project brief so that structural mistakes —
misplaced content, forced splits, missing branches, weak boundaries — are caught
while they are still cheap to fix.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the project brief (scope boundaries, non-goals, primary question), the
   protocol, the parsed scoping discovery (terminology, themes, contradictions,
   methodological differences, missing branches), and the provisional research
   outline to be challenged, section by section.
2. Walk the outline top to bottom. For every top-level section and every
   subsection, challenge it independently. Do not skip items and do not batch
   verdicts.
3. For each item, gather the challenge evidence from the parsed scoping
   discovery: themes or terminology the discovery places elsewhere, contradictions
   the discovery locates in this area, missing branches the discovery flags that
   this item ignores, and scope-boundary statements from the project brief the
   item crosses.
4. Assign exactly one verdict per item, chosen only from this fixed set: KEEP,
   RENAME, MERGE, SPLIT, MOVE, ADD, REMOVE, UNRESOLVED. The meanings are:
   - KEEP: the item is correctly placed and scoped.
   - RENAME: the item's content is right but its title misleads or conflicts with
     the discovery's terminology.
   - MERGE: the item duplicates or overlaps another item and should be combined
     with it.
   - SPLIT: the item mixes two or more separable research topics.
   - MOVE: the item belongs under another parent or at another position.
   - ADD: a new item is required that the outline omits.
   - REMOVE: the item is outside the scope boundaries or unsupported by the
     discovery.
   - UNRESOLVED: the evidence is insufficient to decide; the item needs a human.
5. Write a per-item rationale tied to specific scoping evidence: name the
   discovery finding (theme, contradiction, missing branch, terminology conflict,
   or boundary statement) that motivates the verdict. A verdict without cited
   evidence is not acceptable.
6. For MERGE, SPLIT, and MOVE verdicts, name the target item(s) explicitly. For
   ADD verdicts, propose the new item's title, scope, and expected content. For
   REMOVE verdicts, state what happens to any content the item was expected to
   hold.
7. Do not apply the decisions. This prompt produces a decision list only; a human
   decides every item, and a separate revision prompt applies the approved
   decisions. Where your rationale would require a judgment call, mark the item
   UNRESOLVED rather than deciding it yourself.
8. Close with a summary table of all items, verdicts, and rationales so the human
   can decide each item in one pass.

## Output Contract

```text
OUTLINE CHALLENGE DECISIONS

Item: S01 <title>
Verdict: KEEP | RENAME | MERGE | SPLIT | MOVE | ADD | REMOVE | UNRESOLVED
Rationale: <specific scoping-evidence finding motivating the verdict>
If applicable:
- Rename target: <proposed title>
- Merge target: <item ID>
- Split into: <item IDs with scopes>
- Move target: <new parent or position>
- New item: <title, scope, expected content>

Item: S02 <title>
...

Summary table:
| Item | Verdict | Evidence cited | Decision owner |
| ...  | ...     | ...            | human          |
```

- Allowed verdicts are exactly: KEEP, RENAME, MERGE, SPLIT, MOVE, ADD, REMOVE,
  UNRESOLVED. No other verdict may appear.
- Every outline item receives exactly one verdict and one evidence-tied rationale.
- No decision is applied here; every item is marked as decided by a human.
- No literature, citations, or drafted prose may appear.
