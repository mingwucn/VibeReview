# DR110 — Section-Specific Deep Research (DR110@1.0.0)

## Purpose

Research one exact outline section deeply. The run builds the section's discovery
base: current understanding, primary studies, contradictions, null findings,
qualifications, boundary conditions, alternative explanations and mechanisms,
methodological weaknesses, generalization problems, and candidate claims. Its
output guides retrieval and later claim work for this section only.

## Procedure

1. Read the components appended after this body by the deterministic compiler:
   the project brief (scope boundaries and non-goals), the protocol, the exact
   outline section (its scope and expected-content statements), the focus
   modules declared for this section, and any project supplement. Confine the
   research to the compiled section: content outside the section's stated scope
   belongs to a different run.
2. Use the focus modules' domain emphasis and terminology pointers to recognize
   what matters scientifically in this section and to search under all relevant
   term variants. Use the supplement's project-specific guidance where it
   sharpens the section's questions; do not let it override the outline.
3. Establish the current understanding of the section's topic: what the field
   currently believes, at what level of confidence, and on what kind of evidence.
4. Seek primary studies. For each, record a full bibliographic reference, the
   process and AI task it addresses, its core finding, and its methodological
   basis. Distinguish primary studies from reviews and do not treat reviews as
   independent evidence.
5. Seek contradictions: studies whose findings conflict with each other or with
   the emerging narrative of the section. State each contradiction precisely and
   identify the studies on both sides.
6. Seek null findings: studies that report no effect, no improvement, or no
   advantage where one was expected or claimed. Record them explicitly; a null
   finding is discovery material of equal rank to a positive finding.
7. Seek qualifications and boundary conditions: the limits under which reported
   findings hold — materials, geometries, machines, datasets, regimes, scales —
   and record the boundary conditions in explicit form.
8. Seek alternative explanations and alternative mechanisms: competing accounts
   of the same observed effect, including simpler explanations the main
   narrative overlooks.
9. Seek methodological weaknesses of the studies you cite: evaluation design,
   baselines, data leakage risks, small or single-source datasets, missing
   comparisons, metric choices, and reproducibility concerns.
10. Seek generalization problems: evidence about whether findings transfer
    across machines, materials, scales, datasets, and sites, and record where
    transfer has been tested and where it has only been assumed.
11. Derive candidate claims for this section: cautious, single-assertion
    statements the evidence of this run suggests. Mark them as unverified leads.
12. Actively seek evidence that challenges the outline: findings, contradictions,
    or missing branches that indicate the section's scope, subdivision, or
    expected content is wrong. Record such evidence explicitly so the outline can
    be challenged on return; do not suppress it to fit the outline.
13. Close by stating explicitly that this output is discovery material, not
    canonical evidence, and that every study identification is an unverified lead
    until it passes the authorized evidence pipeline.

Execution boundary: a human operator executes this compiled prompt manually —
copying the exact compiled text into the provider interface without editing,
running the research, and saving the raw report unchanged for VibeReview
registration, which binds the report bytes to this prompt's recorded hash.
No automated execution is part of this procedure.

- Every section above must be present; an empty section must say so explicitly.
- Contradictions, null findings, boundary conditions, alternative explanations,
  methodological weaknesses, and generalization problems must each be sought
  explicitly, not folded into the study list.
- Candidate claims are tentative leads only.
