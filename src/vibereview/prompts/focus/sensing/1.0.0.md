# focus: sensing@1.0.0

## Domain emphasis

In-process sensing is the observational foundation of AI in precision
manufacturing: the models are only as good as the signal that feeds them, and
the gap between what a sensor records and what the process is doing is where
many monitoring and control claims quietly fail. The relevant sensing families
include acoustic emission, vibration, current and voltage, optical and thermal
imaging, and plasma or emission spectroscopy, each with its own bandwidth,
noise, and coupling path to the physical event of interest.

What matters scientifically is signal quality and the demonstrated link from
signal to state. A monitoring model's accuracy is meaningless if the signal
carries the label indirectly — for example, a tool condition inferred from a
feature of the recording setup rather than the process — or if the signal
degrades under industrial conditions of noise, coolant, vibration, and sensor
placement variation. Signal quality claims must state sampling conditions,
sensor placement, and pre-processing; feature-based claims must state what
happens when those features drift.

Sensor fusion is scientifically attractive but methodologically risky: fused
models can outperform single sensors on a fixed dataset while fusing nothing
but correlated noise. Look for fusion studies that ablate the sensors and test
across placements and machines. Also look for the underused pattern: sensing
work evaluated against a physically measured ground truth (wear measured
offline, geometry measured after cut) rather than a proxy label.

Typical methodological weaknesses to watch for: proxy ground truth that is not
independently validated; train/test splits that leak recording sessions;
features that encode the experimental setup; fusion claims without ablation;
and laboratory-signal quality assumed to transfer to the shop floor.

## Terminology pointers

- in-process monitoring, in-situ sensing, process signatures
- acoustic emission (AE), vibration, force, torque, spindle current, power
- optical emission spectroscopy (OES), plasma monitoring, plume imaging
- melt-pool monitoring, pyrometry, infrared / thermal imaging, machine vision
- high-speed imaging, speckle interferometry, confocal measurement
- signal quality, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), sampling rate, aliasing
- feature extraction, time-frequency analysis, wavelets, sensor fusion
- ground truth, proxy labels, drift, calibration, sensor placement
