# focus: autonomy@1.0.0

## Domain emphasis

Autonomy in precision manufacturing means closing the loop: sensing the
process state, deciding, and acting on the process in real time, with
decreasing human supervision. The path runs from fixed-parameter control
through adaptive control and digital twins toward autonomous operation. Each
step is scientifically distinct, and claims should be located precisely on it:
a model that recommends parameters offline is not an adaptive controller, and
an adaptive controller is not an autonomous system.

What matters scientifically is stability, safety, and transfer. Closed-loop
control claims must address stability under the disturbances real processes
present; adaptive control claims must state how adaptation is bounded and what
happens when the model is wrong; digital-twin claims must state how the twin
is kept synchronized with the physical process and how drift is detected. The
recurring barrier across the literature is transfer: controllers and twins
tuned on one machine, material, or regime degrade on another, and the
scientific question is whether the degradation is characterized, bounded, or
merely observed after failure.

Safety and human factors belong in this emphasis: autonomous interventions on
expensive machines and safety-critical parts carry consequences that
laboratory accuracy does not capture. Look for work that validates under
realistic disturbance, that quantifies the cost of wrong decisions, and that
states the conditions under which the autonomy must hand control back.

## Terminology pointers

- closed-loop control, adaptive control, model predictive control (MPC)
- real-time control, in-process adjustment, self-optimization
- digital twin, virtual process model, synchronization, model drift
- autonomy, autonomous manufacturing, self-driving / lights-out manufacturing
- reinforcement learning (RL), safe RL, sim-to-real transfer
- supervisory control, human-in-the-loop, handover, fail-safe
- stability, robustness, disturbance rejection, setpoint tracking
- transfer learning, domain adaptation, cross-machine transfer
