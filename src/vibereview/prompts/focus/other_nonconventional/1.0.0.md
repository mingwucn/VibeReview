# focus: other_nonconventional@1.0.0

## Domain emphasis

Beyond EDM, ECM, and laser machining, non-conventional manufacturing includes
a family of processes whose common feature is material removal or modification
by controlled physical fields rather than by a defined cutting edge:
ultrasonic machining, abrasive jet and abrasive waterjet machining,
electrochemical discharge machining (ECDM), and hybrid processes that combine
field-assisted mechanisms in one operation. These processes matter to a review
as secondary coverage: they complete the process landscape and often contain
the earliest or most instructive instances of a pattern the core processes show
later.

What matters scientifically is mechanism and boundary. Ultrasonic and abrasive
processes are governed by brittle/ductile removal transitions, tool wear, and
slurry or jet dynamics; ECDM combines electrochemical and discharge action at
small scales and is sensitive to gap, electrolyte, and bubble behavior; hybrid
processes claim synergy but must demonstrate it against the sum of their
constituent processes. Coverage of these processes should focus on where their
evidence is strongest — often niche but well-characterized applications — and
on what their methodological profile shares with the core processes: small
datasets, single-machine evidence, and untested transfer.

Typical methodological weaknesses to watch for: synergy claims for hybrid
processes without direct comparison to constituents; ultrasonic and jet
studies extrapolating from model materials to engineering materials without
transition analysis; ECDM studies at laboratory scale generalized to
production claims; and thin literatures where a single research group dominates
the evidence.

## Terminology pointers

- ultrasonic machining (USM), rotary ultrasonic machining (RUM), ultrasonic assisted
- abrasive jet machining (AJM), abrasive waterjet machining (AWJM), waterjet
- electrochemical discharge machining (ECDM), hybrid electrochemical–EDM
- hybrid processes, compound/assisted machining, process combination
- brittle–ductile transition, material removal mechanism, slurry, abrasives
- jet pressure, standoff distance, nozzle wear, striation, taper
- surface integrity, subsurface damage, tool wear, MRR
- micro-machining, glass/ceramic machining, brittle materials

## Boundaries

- This module provides domain emphasis only; it must not draw project-specific
  conclusions or decide what the review claims.
- It must not invent or complete citations, authors, venues, or findings.
- It must not make claims about the field beyond emphasis, terminology, and
  methodological orientation.
