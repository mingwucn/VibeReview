# focus: manufacturing_ai@1.0.0

## Domain emphasis

Artificial intelligence and machine learning in manufacturing span a recurring
set of functions: prediction of process outcomes and quality, optimization of
process parameters and schedules, monitoring of process state, control of
process behavior, and progress toward autonomy. Across all of them, the
scientific questions are similar: what is being predicted or optimized, from
what data, against what baseline, and with what demonstrated benefit over
established practice.

What matters scientifically is not that a model was applied but whether the
study establishes an advantage: evaluation design, comparison against
strong and appropriate baselines, and evidence that gains persist outside the
training distribution. Studies frequently report improvements that shrink or
vanish under stronger baselines, leakage-free evaluation, or transfer to a
different machine, material, or site; such qualifications are often more
scientifically important than the headline improvement.

Look for the recurring structural weaknesses of the area: small or single-source
datasets, train/test splits that leak across time or batches, baselines weaker
than the industrial practice they claim to beat, metrics that do not map to
manufacturing value, and absence of uncertainty estimates or failure analysis.
Also look for the positive pattern: studies that release data or code, that
evaluate across sites or machines, and that analyze when the model fails.
