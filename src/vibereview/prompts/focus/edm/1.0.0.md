# focus: edm@1.0.0

## Domain emphasis

Electrical discharge machining removes material by controlled electrical
discharges in a dielectric gap between electrode and workpiece. The process is
stochastic at the microsecond scale and deterministic only in aggregate, which
makes it a scientifically demanding target for AI: discharge detection and
classification, gap-state estimation, electrode wear compensation, and surface
integrity prediction all depend on sensing and modelling what happens in a gap
that is neither stably observable nor cleanly separable into regimes.

What matters scientifically is the control-relevant state of the discharge
process: normal discharges, arcs, and short circuits differ in their effect on
removal rate and surface damage, and detecting or predicting them is the core
of monitoring and control work. Electrode wear — dimensional and corner wear —
limits accuracy and is a central object of prediction and compensation.
Surface integrity outcomes (roughness, recast layer, microcracks, residual
stress) depend on discharge energy and distribution, and claims about surface
quality must state the energy regime and conditions under which they hold.
Process windows — the stable operating region bounded by arcing, short
circuiting, and insufficient removal — frame every optimization claim.

Typical methodological weaknesses to watch for: studies conducted on a single
machine or power supply whose findings are stated as general; wear or surface
claims measured at settings far from industrial practice; monitoring models
trained and tested on the same discharge populations; and energy- or
efficiency-oriented claims without measured, not assumed, energy accounting.

## Terminology pointers

- electrical discharge machining (EDM), die-sinking EDM, wire EDM (WEDM)
- discharge, spark, arc, short circuit, open circuit; gap voltage and current
- discharge detection / classification, pulse train analysis, gap state estimation
- electrode wear, tool wear compensation, corner wear
- material removal rate (MRR), surface roughness (Ra), recast layer, white layer
- surface integrity, microhardness, residual stress, microcracks
- dielectric fluid, flushing, servo/gap control, pulse on/off time, peak current
- process window, stability, arcing ratio, discharge energy
