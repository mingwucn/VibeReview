# focus: ecm@1.0.0

## Domain emphasis

Electrochemical machining dissolves material anodically in a conductive
electrolyte flowing through a small gap between tool and workpiece. The
process has no tool wear in the mechanical sense and no thermal damage, but it
is governed by an electrochemical, thermal, and hydrodynamic field in the gap
that is hard to observe: interelectrode gap size, electrolyte flow, bubble
evolution, and local current density jointly determine the removal and,
therefore, the shape produced.

What matters scientifically is the gap and the flow. Gap control and pulse
control determine stability and accuracy; electrolyte flow field and its
modelling determine mass transport, heat removal, and the risk of local
boiling or passivation. Shape accuracy is the defining outcome: because
removal is distributed across the gap, predicting and controlling the final
geometry — especially edges and corners — is the central modelling problem.
Process modelling, from equivalent-circuit and analytical models to
finite-element and data-driven hybrids, is scientifically important only insofar
as it is validated against measured geometries under stated conditions.

Typical methodological weaknesses to watch for: geometry predictions validated
on a single material and electrolyte at laboratory scales; flow-field models
without experimental validation of the assumed flow; accuracy claims that omit
the measurement method for the produced shape; and AI-assisted prediction or
control claims benchmarked against weak or unstated baselines.
