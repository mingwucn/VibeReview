# focus: atomic_manufacturing@1.0.0

## Domain emphasis

Atomic and near-atomic manufacturing — the directed placement, removal, or
modification of matter at atomic scales — is a future direction for
manufacturing research, not an established industrial capability. Its
literature mixes demonstrated single-atom and atomic-scale results from
specialized instruments, methodological work that transfers concepts from
established fields, and speculative extrapolation about scalable atomic
manufacturing. The scientific task, everywhere this module applies, is to keep
these categories separate.

The module must require an explicit distinction among three evidence
categories for every relevant statement: demonstrated capability (experimental
results at atomic or near-atomic scale with stated conditions and limits),
plausible methodological transfer (methods whose principles are established
elsewhere and plausibly carry to atomic manufacturing, without demonstrated
atomic-scale deployment), and speculative extrapolation (visions or
projections beyond current demonstrated capability). Statements must be labeled
with the strongest category their evidence supports, and must never be
presented in a stronger category than the evidence allows.

Frame the connection from today's precision non-conventional manufacturing to
atomic manufacturing strictly as a transfer of methods, not of processes:
physics-informed learning, active learning, uncertainty-aware control, inverse
design, autonomous metrology, and Bayesian experimental design are
methodological capabilities that may transfer. Direct process continuity from
EDM, ECM, or laser machining to atomic manufacturing must not be claimed:
these processes operate at fundamentally different scales and mechanisms, and
any statement implying continuity, evolution, or extension of those processes
into atomic manufacturing is prohibited.

## Terminology pointers

- atomic manufacturing, near-atomic manufacturing, atomic-scale fabrication
- atomic manipulation, scanning probe lithography, STM/AFM manipulation
- directed assembly, self-assembly, atomically precise manufacturing
- physics-informed learning, active learning, uncertainty-aware control
- inverse design, autonomous metrology, Bayesian experimental design
- demonstrated capability, methodological transfer, speculative extrapolation
- future outlook, roadmap, horizon, vision paper

## Boundaries

- This module provides domain emphasis only; it must not draw project-specific
  conclusions or decide what the review claims.
- It must not invent or complete citations, authors, venues, or findings.
- It must not make claims about the field beyond emphasis, terminology, and
  methodological orientation.
- It must require the demonstrated-capability / plausible-methodological-transfer
  / speculative-extrapolation distinction for every relevant statement.
- It must frame the connection to atomic manufacturing as transfer of methods
  only (physics-informed learning, active learning, uncertainty-aware control,
  inverse design, autonomous metrology, Bayesian experimental design).
- It must prohibit claiming direct process continuity from EDM, ECM, or laser
  machining to atomic manufacturing.
