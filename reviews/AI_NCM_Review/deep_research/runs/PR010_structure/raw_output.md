SCIENTIFIC STRUCTURE CANDIDATES

Candidate 1 — process-centered:

* Organizing unit: process family

* Main parts (in order):

  1. Common analytical frame for AI-assisted precision non-conventional manufacturing
  2. Electrical discharge machining (EDM)
  3. Electrochemical machining (ECM)
  4. Laser-based precision manufacturing
  5. Secondary-process comparative synthesis
  6. Cross-process synthesis of robustness, transferability, control, and autonomy
  7. Outlook toward atomic manufacturing

* Internal logic of the three core-process parts:

  * Each core process is treated as a distinct physical and manufacturing system.
  * Within each process family, AI applications are organized using the same functional sequence:

    * process-response prediction and surrogate/data-driven modelling;
    * parameter selection and optimization;
    * sensing, signal interpretation, monitoring, and classification;
    * adaptive decision-making and feedback control;
    * progression toward process autonomy.
  * Methodological and validation limitations are examined within each process family before cross-process comparison.

* Mapping to project brief:

  * EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing form the three principal units of synthesis and therefore retain the primary emphasis required by the project brief.
  * Ultrasonic machining, abrasive jet machining, waterjet and abrasive-waterjet machining, ECDM, and hybrid non-conventional manufacturing are consolidated into a secondary comparative part rather than receiving coverage equivalent to the core processes.
  * Secondary processes are used for comparison, boundary testing, and examination of whether organizational patterns established for the core processes can be applied across different process mechanisms.
  * AI functions specified in the project brief are represented repeatedly within each core process.
  * Technical, methodological, data-related, and validation barriers are first treated process-by-process and subsequently compared across processes.
  * Atomic manufacturing appears only in the final outlook and is not integrated into the principal evidence synthesis.

* Structural strengths:

  * Preserves distinctions among fundamentally different manufacturing mechanisms.
  * Makes the primary-versus-secondary process hierarchy immediately visible.
  * Provides a stable location for process-specific sensing, variables, outputs, validation conditions, and control requirements.
  * Offers a clear reading path for readers approaching the review from a particular manufacturing process.

* Structural limitations:

  * Prediction, optimization, monitoring, control, and autonomy must recur in several process parts.
  * Repeated treatment of the same AI function creates a risk of structural duplication.
  * Cross-process methodological similarities and differences may become visible only after substantial process-specific discussion.
  * The progression from predictive modelling toward closed-loop intelligence is not itself the principal organizing axis.

Candidate 2 — AI-function-centered:

* Organizing unit: AI function

* Main parts (in order):

  1. Prediction and data-driven/surrogate process modelling
  2. Parameter selection and optimization
  3. Condition, state, defect, anomaly, and quality monitoring
  4. Sensor-data interpretation and fusion
  5. Adaptive decision-making and feedback/closed-loop control
  6. Increasing levels of process autonomy
  7. Cross-functional methodological and validation constraints
  8. Secondary-process comparison and boundary testing
  9. Outlook toward atomic manufacturing

* Internal logic:

  * Each AI-function part compares applications across EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing.
  * Process family acts as a secondary analytical variable within each functional part.
  * Evidence from secondary processes is introduced selectively where it supports comparison or tests whether an AI-function pattern can be meaningfully considered beyond the three core processes.
  * Technical and methodological constraints are associated with the relevant AI function and subsequently integrated across the functional sequence.

* Mapping to project brief:

  * Prediction, optimization, monitoring, control, and autonomy directly mirror the functional sequence specified in the primary review question.
  * EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing are represented within every functional unit for which relevant evidence is available.
  * Secondary processes remain comparative rather than co-equal process families.
  * Cross-functional methodological constraints provide a location for data quality, validation, robustness, transferability, and related limitations.
  * Atomic manufacturing remains isolated in the outlook.

* Structural strengths:

  * Directly reflects the functional wording of the primary review question.
  * Makes the conceptual progression from prediction toward control and autonomy explicit.
  * Facilitates direct comparison of how the same AI function is instantiated across different manufacturing processes.
  * Reduces repetition of generic AI-function definitions.

* Structural limitations:

  * Individual process families become fragmented across multiple parts.
  * Process-specific physical constraints may require repeated contextual explanation.
  * Differences in variables, sensing strategies, process states, objectives, and control actions may be separated from the process context needed to interpret them.
  * Secondary-process evidence may be forced into function-based categories even when its primary value is process-level comparison.
  * A function-centered arrangement may encourage superficial comparison unless process-specific validation conditions are repeatedly reconstructed.

Candidate 3 — hybrid:

* Organizing unit: process family with cross-cutting AI-function and methodological themes

* Main parts (in order):

  1. Shared analytical framework
  2. EDM as a core process family
  3. ECM as a core process family
  4. Laser-based precision manufacturing as a core process family
  5. Secondary-process comparative synthesis
  6. Cross-process synthesis by AI function
  7. Cross-cutting methodological synthesis
  8. Progression from isolated AI functions toward closed-loop manufacturing intelligence
  9. Outlook toward atomic manufacturing

* Common analytical axes applied within the three core-process parts:

  * prediction and surrogate/data-driven modelling;
  * parameter selection and optimization;
  * monitoring, classification, and sensor interpretation;
  * adaptive decision-making and control;
  * autonomy-related functions;
  * validation conditions and limitations relevant to the process family.

* Cross-cutting AI-function themes:

  * prediction and modelling;
  * optimization;
  * monitoring and state/quality interpretation;
  * sensing and sensor fusion;
  * adaptive decision-making;
  * feedback and closed-loop control;
  * autonomy.

* Cross-cutting methodological themes:

  * data provenance, representativeness, and sufficiency;
  * sensing and observability;
  * validation design;
  * robustness under changing process conditions;
  * physical interpretability and physics–data integration;
  * cross-machine, cross-material, and cross-condition transferability;
  * integration of prediction, optimization, monitoring, and control;
  * distinction between offline computational performance and closed-loop manufacturing intelligence.

* Mapping to project brief:

  * EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing remain the principal organizational units and receive primary coverage.
  * All AI functions named in the project brief are applied as common analytical axes across the three core-process families.
  * Ultrasonic machining, abrasive jet machining, waterjet and abrasive-waterjet machining, ECDM, and hybrid non-conventional processes are grouped into a dedicated comparative synthesis rather than treated as equivalent to the core processes.
  * Secondary processes are used to test the boundaries of cross-process interpretations and methodological patterns.
  * Technical, methodological, data-related, and validation barriers are examined initially in their process-specific context and then compared explicitly across process families.
  * Robustness, transferability, closed-loop control, and autonomy are treated as integrative questions rather than being inferred directly from predictive performance.
  * Atomic manufacturing is separated from the evidence-bearing structure and confined to the outlook.

* Rationale for cross-cutting placement of themes:

  * Data, sensing, validation, robustness, physics–data integration, transferability, and autonomy affect more than one process family and more than one AI function.
  * Embedding complete treatments of these themes independently inside EDM, ECM, and laser sections would require repeated analytical frameworks and would make direct methodological comparison less explicit.
  * Removing them entirely from the process sections would also be unsuitable because their interpretation depends on process-specific variables, mechanisms, sensing configurations, and validation contexts.
  * The hybrid structure therefore uses a two-stage treatment: process-specific manifestations are retained within each core-process synthesis, whereas comparative interpretation is reserved for dedicated cross-cutting synthesis.
  * This arrangement keeps process physics as the primary context while allowing identical methodological questions to be applied across processes.

Candidate 1 — process-centered:

* Criterion 1: Strong alignment is achieved because the three core processes remain central, although the AI-functional progression in the primary question becomes subordinate to process organization.
* Criterion 2: Scope boundaries are represented directly and transparently, particularly the distinction between core and secondary processes.
* Criterion 3: Process-specific critical synthesis is well supported, but later cross-process integration is required to prevent the review from becoming a sequence of semi-independent process surveys.
* Criterion 4: Contradictions and methodological differences can be examined effectively within each process, but cross-process contradictions are less immediately visible.
* Criterion 5: Repetition is the principal structural weakness because prediction, optimization, monitoring, control, and autonomy recur within each core process.
* Criterion 6: Reader navigation is straightforward because process families provide familiar and unambiguous organizational units.

Candidate 2 — AI-function-centered:

* Criterion 1: The structure directly reflects the functional wording of the primary review question, especially the progression from prediction through optimization and monitoring to control and autonomy.
* Criterion 2: The process hierarchy is less explicit because EDM, ECM, and laser-based manufacturing are distributed across multiple functional parts.
* Criterion 3: Functional comparison supports critical synthesis, but preservation of process-specific context requires repeated qualification.
* Criterion 4: Methodological differences associated with a common AI function can be compared directly, which is a substantial strength.
* Criterion 5: Forced comparison becomes possible when different processes, datasets, sensing conditions, outputs, or validation designs are placed under a common functional heading despite limited comparability.
* Criterion 6: The conceptual sequence is clear, but readers interested in a specific process must reconstruct that process across several parts.

Candidate 3 — hybrid:

* Criterion 1: Both dimensions of the primary question are retained: the three core processes remain primary units while prediction, optimization, monitoring, control, and autonomy become explicit common analytical axes.
* Criterion 2: Core, secondary, and outlook-only process categories can be maintained without assigning equivalent structural weight to them.
* Criterion 3: Process-specific evidence can first be interpreted within its physical and methodological context and subsequently synthesized across common analytical dimensions, matching the requirements of a critical narrative review with structured evidence synthesis.
* Criterion 4: Differences can be examined at two levels: within individual process families and across process families under identical functional and methodological questions.
* Criterion 5: Some deliberate revisiting of material is required, but the distinction between process-specific synthesis and cross-process interpretation limits unnecessary duplication and reduces forced one-to-one comparison.
* Criterion 6: The structure is more complex than the purely process-centered alternative, but the hierarchy remains explicit if the same analytical axes are consistently applied to EDM, ECM, and laser-based manufacturing.

RECOMMENDATION

Recommended structure: Candidate 3 — hybrid

Justification:

Candidate 3 provides the closest structural correspondence to the primary review question because that question contains two inseparable dimensions: process families and AI functions. A purely process-centered structure preserves the first dimension but subordinates the second, whereas a purely function-centered structure foregrounds AI functions at the cost of fragmenting the physical and experimental context of the manufacturing processes. The hybrid arrangement preserves both.

Against Criterion 1, the hybrid structure directly represents EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing while applying prediction, optimization, monitoring, control, and autonomy as common analytical axes. This prevents either the manufacturing processes or the AI functions from becoming merely contextual.

Against Criterion 2, the hierarchy specified in the project brief remains intact. The three core processes form the principal synthesis; ultrasonic machining, abrasive jet machining, waterjet and abrasive-waterjet machining, ECDM, and hybrid processes remain comparative; atomic manufacturing remains outside the principal evidence synthesis.

Against Criterion 3, the structure supports critical narrative synthesis because individual studies can be interpreted within process-specific technical contexts before broader methodological relationships are synthesized. Structured evidence synthesis can therefore be applied consistently without requiring heterogeneous results to be quantitatively pooled.

Against Criterion 4, contradictions and qualifications can be exposed both within a process family and across process families. Differences associated with process mechanism, data source, sensing strategy, validation design, operating conditions, and AI function can therefore remain visible rather than being collapsed into a single functional category.

Against Criterion 5, the hybrid design provides a controlled form of repetition. Process sections establish context-specific evidence, whereas cross-cutting sections perform comparison rather than reproducing the same descriptions. The distinction between evidence presentation and comparative interpretation should limit duplication.

Against Criterion 6, the process-first hierarchy provides an accessible primary navigation structure. The later cross-cutting synthesis then provides a second analytical layer without requiring readers to reconstruct each process from multiple disconnected AI-function sections.

The cross-cutting themes should therefore remain outside the three principal process sections as dedicated synthesis dimensions. Data and sensing, validation, robustness, physics–data integration, transferability, closed-loop control, and autonomy are not properties of a single process family. Their comparative value arises from asking the same methodological questions across EDM, ECM, laser-based manufacturing, and, where appropriate, the secondary processes. Process-specific manifestations should still be identified inside the relevant process synthesis, but their comparative interpretation should occur at the cross-cutting level.

Why the strongest alternative was not chosen:

Candidate 1 — process-centered is the strongest alternative because it offers the clearest process hierarchy, preserves physical context, and maps directly onto the distinction between core and secondary process families. It was not selected because its principal organizing unit does not represent the complete logic of the primary review question. Prediction, optimization, monitoring, control, and autonomy would have to be treated independently within EDM, ECM, and laser-based manufacturing, producing repeated functional structures and postponing explicit comparison of methodological differences until the end of the review.

Candidate 3 retains the process-centered strengths of Candidate 1 while introducing a controlled second analytical layer for AI functions and methodological themes. It therefore provides stronger support for examining the transition from individual AI applications toward robustness, transferability, closed-loop control, and increasing autonomy without treating heterogeneous process families as directly interchangeable.

Constraint and non-goal check:

* The structure does not imply systematic-search completeness.
* No meta-analysis or quantitative pooling is built into the structure.
* No section requires the corpus to be treated as exhaustive.
* No universal research-gap claims are required or implied.
* No predictive-performance result is structurally equated with robustness, physical validity, transferability, industrial readiness, or autonomy.
* Cross-process comparison is included without assuming direct transferability between process families.
* Conventional machining, additive manufacturing, and general smart-manufacturing research are not introduced as independent review domains.
* Secondary processes remain comparative and do not receive equivalent status to EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing.
* Atomic manufacturing remains restricted to the outlook and is not represented as an established continuation of the reviewed process families.
* No literature-derived claim, citation, performance value, or statement about the state of the field is required at this stage.
* MyLib remains the designated canonical evidence source for later evidence-bearing stages; nothing produced by this structure-generation stage is treated as canonical evidence.
* Deep Research outputs are not incorporated into the scientific structure as evidence.
* The recommended structure is therefore consistent with the project brief and protocol without modification of their constraints.
