## Organizing unit

Process family with cross-cutting AI-function and methodological themes.

## Main parts (in order)

1. Shared analytical framework
2. EDM as a core process family
3. ECM as a core process family
4. Laser-based precision manufacturing as a core process family
5. Secondary-process comparative synthesis
6. Cross-process synthesis by AI function
7. Cross-cutting methodological synthesis
8. Progression from isolated AI functions toward closed-loop manufacturing intelligence
9. Outlook toward atomic manufacturing

## Common analytical axes applied within the three core-process parts

* prediction and surrogate/data-driven modelling;
* parameter selection and optimization;
* monitoring, classification, and sensor interpretation;
* adaptive decision-making and control;
* autonomy-related functions;
* validation conditions and limitations relevant to the process family.

## Cross-cutting AI-function themes

* prediction and modelling;
* optimization;
* monitoring and state/quality interpretation;
* sensing and sensor fusion;
* adaptive decision-making;
* feedback and closed-loop control;
* autonomy.

## Cross-cutting methodological themes

* data provenance, representativeness, and sufficiency;
* sensing and observability;
* validation design;
* robustness under changing process conditions;
* physical interpretability and physics-data integration;
* cross-machine, cross-material, and cross-condition transferability;
* integration of prediction, optimization, monitoring, and control;
* distinction between offline computational performance and closed-loop manufacturing intelligence.

## Mapping to project brief

* EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing remain the principal organizational units and receive primary coverage.
* All AI functions named in the project brief are applied as common analytical axes across the three core-process families.
* Ultrasonic machining, abrasive jet machining, waterjet and abrasive-waterjet machining, ECDM, and hybrid non-conventional processes are grouped into a dedicated comparative synthesis rather than treated as equivalent to the core processes.
* Secondary processes are used to test the boundaries of cross-process interpretations and methodological patterns.
* Technical, methodological, data-related, and validation barriers are examined initially in their process-specific context and then compared explicitly across process families.
* Robustness, transferability, closed-loop control, and autonomy are treated as integrative questions rather than being inferred directly from predictive performance.
* Atomic manufacturing is separated from the evidence-bearing structure and confined to the outlook.

## Rationale for cross-cutting placement of themes

Data, sensing, validation, robustness, physics-data integration, transferability,
and autonomy affect more than one process family and more than one AI function.
The hybrid structure uses a two-stage treatment: process-specific manifestations
are retained within each core-process synthesis, whereas comparative
interpretation is reserved for dedicated cross-cutting synthesis. This keeps
process physics as the primary context while allowing identical methodological
questions to be applied across processes.

## Why the strongest alternative was not chosen

Candidate 1 (process-centered, 26.5/30) offers the clearest process hierarchy and
preserves physical context, but its organizing unit does not represent the
complete logic of the primary review question: prediction, optimization,
monitoring, control, and autonomy would have to be treated independently within
each core process, producing repeated functional structures and postponing
explicit methodological comparison until the end of the review. Candidate 3
retains Candidate 1's strengths while adding a controlled second analytical
layer for AI functions and methodological themes.

## Constraint and non-goal check (verbatim from the run output)

* The structure does not imply systematic-search completeness.
* No meta-analysis or quantitative pooling is built into the structure.
* No section requires the corpus to be treated as exhaustive.
* No universal research-gap claims are required or implied.
* No predictive-performance result is structurally equated with robustness, physical validity, transferability, industrial readiness, or autonomy.
* Cross-process comparison is included without assuming direct transferability between process families.
* Conventional machining, additive manufacturing, and general smart-manufacturing research are not introduced as independent review domains.
* Secondary processes remain comparative and do not receive equivalent status to EDM, ECM, and laser-based precision manufacturing.
* Atomic manufacturing remains restricted to the outlook and is not represented as an established continuation of the reviewed process families.
* No literature-derived claim, citation, performance value, or statement about the state of the field is required at this stage.
* MyLib remains the designated canonical evidence source for later evidence-bearing stages; nothing produced by this structure-generation stage is treated as canonical evidence.
* Deep Research outputs are not incorporated into the scientific structure as evidence.
* The recommended structure is therefore consistent with the project brief and protocol without modification of their constraints.
